  <p class="academic-intro">
    My research lies at the intersection of labour economics, applied
    microeconometrics, and gender economics. I use
    longitudinal and administrative data to study how labour-market
    institutions and public policies shape employment, earnings, workplace
    flexibility, and gender inequality.
  </p>

  <article class="academic-item">
    <h2 class="academic-item__title">
      The Trade-Off Between Union Wage Gains and Working-Time Flexibility
    </h2>


    <div class="academic-item__description">
      <p>
        This project examines whether the wage benefits associated with union
        membership are partly offset by reduced access to flexible working
        arrangements.
      </p>

      <p>
        Using HILDA longitudinal data from 2001–2022, the study applies
        instrumental-variable estimation and a mediation framework to examine
        differences across gender, income groups, and family structures.
      </p>
    </div>

  <article class="academic-item">
    <h2 class="academic-item__title">
      The Effect of New Zealand’s 20 Hours Free Early Childhood Education Policy on Gender Pay Gap
    </h2>


    <div class="academic-item__description">
      <p>
        This project evaluates the effects of New Zealand’s 20 Hours Free early
        childhood education policy on maternal labour-force participation, 
        earnings and gender pay gap.
      </p>

      <p>
        It uses linked administrative data and event-study and dynamic
        difference-in-differences methods. Data processing and empirical
        analysis are conducted using SQL and Stata.
      </p>
    </div>
  </article>
